Choosing where to sleep in Albania is a bigger decision than in most countries, because the distances are short but the roads are slow, and because the difference between two towns forty minutes apart can be the difference between a holiday you loved and one you tolerated.

Sarandë and Himarë are both on the Riviera. They are not remotely the same trip. Blloku and the Grand Park are both central Tirana. One of them will keep you awake.

So this is not a hotel list. It is a guide to picking the right base, region by region, with the honest trade-offs of each.

## The Four Kinds of Albanian Accommodation

Worth knowing what you are choosing between, because the categories do not map onto western European ones.

**Hotels** are what you expect, concentrated in Tirana, Durrës, Sarandë and Vlorë. Three-star here is generally cleaner and newer than three-star in Italy, because most of the building stock is under fifteen years old.

**Apartments** dominate the coast. Family-owned, often the top floor of a house, frequently better value than a hotel, and the standard varies enormously. Photographs are usually honest; the location described as "sea view, 5 min beach" is often 5 minutes uphill.

**Bujtina** (guesthouses) are the Alps and the rural interior. Family homes with rooms, almost always half board, and the meal is not incidental to the experience. Thirty to forty-five euro per person including dinner and breakfast is the going rate in Theth and Valbona.

**Agroturizëm** is the working farm stay, and Albania's fastest-growing accommodation category. You sleep where the food is grown, and I have written about [what that actually looks like](/blog/albanian-agroturizem-farm-to-table/). The good ones are inland and require a car.

## Tirana: Which Neighbourhood

Two nights minimum, and the neighbourhood matters more than the star rating.

**Blloku.** The former closed quarter of the communist elite, now the dining and nightlife district. Walkable to everything, best restaurant density in the country, and the most expensive beds in the city. **It is also loud until two in the morning at weekends.** If you sleep lightly, do not book a street-facing room here in summer. If you are here for two nights and want the city at full volume, this is the right choice.

**Around Pazari i Ri.** My usual recommendation for a first visit. Central, ten minutes on foot from Skanderbeg Square, the best food in the city at the most reasonable prices, and quieter at night than Blloku without being dead. The market square in the morning is worth the location on its own.

**The Grand Park and Artificial Lake.** Green, quiet, family-friendly, twenty minutes' walk from the centre. Choose it if you have children, if you run in the mornings, or if the thought of Blloku on a Saturday makes you tired.

**Komuna e Parisit.** Residential, well connected, noticeably cheaper, and where you end up if you are staying more than a week. Not scenic. Genuinely liveable.

**Avoid** anything advertised as central that turns out to be beyond the ring road. Tirana's centre is small and walkable; twenty minutes out of it, you are dependent on taxis and the value evaporates.

## The Riviera: The Decision That Matters Most

This is where people get it wrong, so let me be blunt about each.

### Sarandë

The Riviera's city. A horseshoe bay stacked with apartment blocks, a long promenade, the Corfu ferry, and by far the best transport links in the south. Butrint is thirty minutes away, Ksamil thirty, the Blue Eye twenty-five.

**Stay here if** you are not driving. Sarandë is the one Riviera base that works properly without a car, and that is a real advantage.

**Do not stay here** expecting a beautiful town. The beaches are serviceable, the town beach is crowded, and the architecture is unplanned concrete. It is a base, not a destination.

Roughly 45 to 90 euro for a decent double in season.

### Himarë

The best all-round base on the coast, and what I recommend most often. A genuine small town with a permanent population and its own life, sitting at the midpoint of the Riviera, with more than a dozen beaches within reach from lively town sand to coves you need a boat for.

Prices run 45 to 120 euro depending on season and how close to the water you are, and it is consistently better value than Sarandë or Ksamil for what you get.

**Stay here if** you want to swim in a different place every day and eat somewhere that is not exclusively serving tourists.

### Dhërmi

Polished, social, and priced accordingly, at 60 to 150 euro. Beach clubs, summer festivals, the best-looking hotel stock on the coast, and Gjipe beach within reach.

**Stay here if** the beach club scene is the point. **Avoid it if** you want a quiet week; in August this is the loudest stretch of coast in Albania.

### Ksamil

White sand, turquoise shallows, photogenic islets, and in August a crowd density that has to be seen. It has been comprehensively developed and priced as a resort.

**Stay here in June or September** and it is genuinely lovely. **Stay here in August** and you will spend the week wondering what everyone was talking about. I have written a whole piece on [what happened to Ksamil](/blog/the-enchanting-beach-of-ksamil-a-hidden-gem-in-albania/), and the honest alternatives are in [the southern beaches guide](/blog/must-visit-southern-albanian-beaches-top-riviera-locations/).

### Vlorë

Rarely recommended and worth a mention. It is a real city with a real waterfront, cheaper than anywhere south of it, and thirty minutes from where the Riviera properly begins. Not beautiful. Very practical.

**The car question on the Riviera is decisive.** Sarandë works without one. Himarë and Dhërmi technically work without one, in that buses exist, but the entire point of those bases is reaching the coves between the towns, and that needs wheels. ## The Alps: Bujtina, and How Booking Actually Works

Theth and Valbona are guesthouse country and the model is the same in both: a family house, a room, dinner at a shared table, breakfast before you walk, thirty to forty-five euro a head.

**The food is the reason to do it this way.** Mountain trout, homemade cheese, bread from a wood oven, and raki that will not have travelled more than fifty metres. A hotel in these valleys would be worse in every way that matters.

**Book ahead in July and August.** Both valleys genuinely fill, and neither has a fallback within two hours.

**Book direct where you can.** Many bujtina take reservations by phone or WhatsApp and never appear on the big platforms at all, which is also where the better prices are. That does mean having working data from the moment you land, since these conversations happen in real time and not by email; [the connectivity options are here](/blog/albania-sim-card-esim/), and <a class="affil" href="https://tp.media/r?campaign_id=541&marker=770798&p=8310&trs=567396&u=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.airalo.com%2Falbania-esim&sub_id=where-to-stay-in-albania" target="_blank" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener">an eSIM sorted before you fly</a> is the least painful version.

**Theth or Valbona?** Theth has more to do without walking: the Blue Eye, the waterfall, the lock-in tower. Valbona is greener, gentler, and better if you want one big day. Most people do both, connected by [the pass hike](/blog/valbona-to-theth-hike/), which is the right answer.

## The Ottoman Towns

**Berat.** Stay in Gorica, across the river from the famous Mangalem facade, in a restored Ottoman house. You get the view rather than being in it, the walk across the bridge at night is one of the pleasures of the town, and the prices are lower. Forty to seventy euro.

**Gjirokastër.** Stay inside the old town, uphill, and accept that you will be walking on stone at an angle. The restored tower houses are the accommodation here and they are remarkable. Do not book something modern down in the new town to save fifteen euro; you will have missed the point of the place.

**Shkodër.** The gateway to the north, and worth a night in its own right rather than a 6am start from Tirana. Cheap, easy, and the natural staging post for the Koman ferry.

## The Rules I Would Give a Friend

**Fewer bases, longer stays.** Two nights minimum everywhere. Albania punishes packing and unpacking more than most countries because the transfers are slower than they look.

**Book the coast early for August and the Alps early for all summer.** Everything else can be left late.

**Off season, book nothing more than a few days out.** Prices in June and September fall for last-minute rooms, not rise.

**Read the location, not the town.** "Ksamil" covers a peninsula. "Himarë" covers fifteen kilometres of coast. Check where on a map before you commit, especially if you are not driving.

**Expect to pay cash.** Many family-run places prefer it, some quietly discount for it, and card machines in rural guesthouses are still not universal.
