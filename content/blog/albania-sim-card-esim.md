Here is the single most expensive misunderstanding in Albanian travel, and it has nothing to do with taxis or restaurants.

**Albania is not in the EU roaming zone.** Your phone does not know that. It will connect to Vodafone Albania or One Albania the moment you cross the border from Greece or step off the ferry from Italy, and it will keep doing everything it did yesterday, at rates that can run into euros per megabyte depending on your provider. People discover this in the second week, on the bill, and it is a genuinely bad way to find out.

The good news is that solving it costs somewhere between three and thirty euro depending on how you do it, and takes about five minutes. This is that five minutes.

## The Roaming Situation, Precisely

Three separate things get confused here, so let me separate them.

**EU Roam Like At Home does not cover Albania.** Not in 2026. The European Commission proposed extending the scheme to the Western Balkans in February 2026, and negotiations are authorised, but a proposal is not a policy. Until something is actually adopted and in force, your German or Italian or Irish plan treats Albania as a foreign country, because it is one.

**The Western Balkans have their own agreement, and it is real.** Since July 2021, a SIM from any of the six, meaning Albania, Kosovo, Montenegro, Serbia, North Macedonia and Bosnia and Herzegovina, roams across all six at domestic rates. This matters enormously if you are doing a Balkan circuit. Buy one Albanian SIM and it works in Kosovo and Montenegro too, at no extra cost.

**Some European operators do include Albania voluntarily.** A handful of plans bundle the Western Balkans as a goodwill zone. Check yours before you assume anything, and check the actual terms rather than the marketing page. The phrase you are looking for is Albania by name, not "Europe".

## What a Local SIM Costs in 2026

Two operators matter. **Vodafone Albania** and **One Albania**, the latter being what you get after ALBtelecom and the old Telekom Albania merged. Coverage between them is broadly comparable and both have desks in the arrivals hall at Rinas, open around the clock, taking cash or card.

You will need your passport. SIM registration is mandatory here and the shop will scan it.

| Vodafone tourist | 40 GB + 1,000 min | 15 days | 2,300 lekë (~23 EUR) |
| Vodafone tourist | 100 GB + unlimited min | 21 days | 2,900 lekë (~29 EUR) |
| One Tourist Pro | 40 GB + 1,000 min | 21 days | 2,600 lekë (~26 EUR) |
| One Tourist Ultra | 100 GB + 4,000 min | 21 days | 2,900 lekë (~29 EUR) |

Those are good numbers if you look at them per gigabyte. One hundred gigabytes for twenty-nine euro is more data than almost any European domestic plan gives you, and if you are here for three weeks, working remotely, or tethering a laptop, the local SIM is straightforwardly the best value available and I would take it.

**But look at what you are actually buying.** Most people on a nine-day trip use four or five gigabytes. Paying twenty-nine euro for a hundred is not a bargain, it is a rounding error you have chosen to fund. And the minutes, which look generous, are national minutes, useful for calling a guesthouse in Theth and useless for calling home.

## When an eSIM Is the Better Answer

For a normal one or two week holiday, an eSIM usually wins, and not only on price.

You install it before you fly. It activates when you land. There is no queue in arrivals at eleven at night, no passport scan, no plastic tray, and your home number stays live in the other slot so your bank's verification codes still arrive. That last point is worth more than people expect.

So, plainly:

- **Under two weeks, moderate use, want it working on landing:** eSIM.
- **Three weeks or more, tethering, working, or you need a local number people can call:** local SIM from Vodafone or One.
- **Multi-country Balkan trip:** either an Albanian physical SIM, which roams free across the six, or a regional eSIM. Both work. The physical SIM is better value if you are moving slowly.

**Check your phone takes an eSIM before you buy one.** Every recent iPhone and most recent Android flagships do. Older and budget handsets often do not, and a purchased eSIM you cannot install is not refundable in any way that will make you happy.

## Where the Signal Actually Dies

This is the part the comparison sites cannot tell you, because it takes living here.

**Tirana, Durrës, Vlorë, Sarandë, Shkodër, Korçë and the main roads between them:** fine. 4G at 15 to 40 Mbps, reliably. There is commercial 5G in parts of Tirana and one or two other cities, but treat it as a bonus rather than a plan.

**The Riviera:** good in the villages, patchy on the road between them, and genuinely absent in some of the coves you will want to reach. Download your map before you drive over Llogara.

**The Alps:** this is where it matters. Theth and Valbona both have workable coverage in the villages now, which was not true a decade ago. The pass between them does not, in long stretches. If you are walking [the Valbona to Theth route](/blog/valbona-to-theth-hike/), you should be carrying an offline map regardless of which SIM you bought, and you should not be relying on a phone call to sort out a problem at the top.

**The far northeast and the Korab border zone:** assume nothing.

## Practical Details People Get Wrong

**Wifi is everywhere and it is decent.** Albanian cafes have offered free wifi since long before it was normal in western Europe, and the password is usually written on the wall or given without being asked. This genuinely reduces how much mobile data you need. A day spent in Tirana bouncing between a coffee at ten and a lunch at two is a day on someone else's connection.

**Buy data before you need a taxi.** Neither Uber nor Bolt operates in Albania. The local apps, Patoko and Clust, are what people actually use, and they need data to work. Landing without connectivity means paying the fixed rank price at the airport, which I have broken down in [the guide to getting out of Rinas](/blog/tirana-airport-transport/).

**Top-ups are trivial.** Every kiosk, every corner shop, every petrol station sells credit for both operators. You will not be stranded.

**Do not buy a SIM from a street seller.** The registration requirement is real, and an unregistered SIM can be cut off without warning. Use an operator shop or an airport desk.

**Turn off roaming on your home SIM before you land**, not after. Set it in the taxi queue at your departure airport, because the moment your plane doors open in Tirana your phone starts looking for a network, and modern phones sync in the background whether you asked them to or not.
