Almost everyone who comes to Albania arrives at the same place. Tirana International Airport, still called Rinas by everyone who lives here, after the village it sits next to. It handles the overwhelming majority of the country's air traffic, and for most visitors the seventeen kilometres between its doors and central Tirana are the first piece of Albanian logistics they have to solve.

It is not a difficult problem. But it is one where the difference between the cheapest and the most expensive answer is roughly tenfold, and where a surprising number of people pay the top price without knowing the others exist.

## First, Where You Actually Are

Rinas sits about 17 kilometres northwest of Tirana, which is roughly 25 minutes of driving when the road is clear and 45 when it is not. It is a single terminal. Arrivals and departures are on the same level, the walk between them is about ninety seconds, and there is no train.

That last point matters more than it sounds. Albania has almost no functioning passenger rail, so everything below is a road option. There is no fast link to skip the traffic on the Tirana ring road, and no version of this journey where you arrive in the centre in ten minutes.

**Before you leave the terminal, do two things.** Take cash from an ATM, because a lot of what you will pay for in the next few days wants lekë and not euro. And if you want a local number, the Vodafone and One Albania desks are in arrivals, though there is a better answer to that problem which I get to further down.

## The Five Ways Out

| Option | Cost | Time to city centre | Runs when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rinas Express bus | 400 lekë (about 4 EUR) | 25 to 35 min | 24 hours, hourly |
| Taxi app (Patoko, Clust) | 1,200 to 1,800 lekë | 25 min | App-dependent |
| Official airport taxi | 23 EUR centre, 28 EUR outskirts | 20 to 25 min | 24 hours |
| Pre-booked private transfer | 20 to 35 EUR | 25 min | Booked ahead |
| Rental car | From 15 EUR/day plus fuel | 25 min | Desk hours |

### The bus, which is the right answer more often than not

The **Rinas Express** is operated by a company called LU-NA and runs between the airport and central Tirana around the clock, roughly once an hour, for **400 lekë a person**. Luggage is free. The coaches are modern, air conditioned, and have a proper hold underneath, so this is not a case of wrestling a suitcase onto a minibus.

It stops behind the National Theatre of Opera and Ballet, which puts you a two-minute walk from Skanderbeg Square and inside walking distance of a large share of Tirana's hotels and apartments.

I take it myself when I come back from Italy. At four euro for a half-hour ride into the middle of a capital city, it is one of the genuinely good deals in Albanian travel, and the fact that it runs at three in the morning removes most of the argument for anything else.

### The official taxi, and why it costs what it costs

The airport has an exclusive taxi concession, currently held by Auto Holiday Albania. The fare is fixed and published: **23 euro to central Tirana, 28 euro to the outer neighbourhoods.** It is not a scam and there is no meter to argue about. It is simply the price of the only cars allowed to wait at the rank.

Fixed pricing is worth something. You will not be quoted one number and charged another, which is not a guarantee you get everywhere in the Balkans. But you are paying roughly six times the bus fare for ten minutes of saved time and a door-to-door drop.

Take it if you land with three suitcases and two tired children. Otherwise you are buying convenience at a heavy markup.

### Taxi apps, which is where the locals are

**Neither Uber nor Bolt operates in Albania.** People arrive expecting one of them, find nothing, and conclude the only option is the rank. That is the single most common and most expensive mistake at this airport.

What does work are the Albanian apps. **Patoko** is usually cheapest. **Clust** has an English interface and card payment, which makes it the easier of the two for a first-time visitor. Either will typically quote **1,200 to 1,800 lekë** from the airport, so 12 to 18 euro, with the price shown before you accept.

The catch is that you need data to summon one, which brings us back to the connectivity question. An [eSIM installed before you fly](/blog/albania-sim-card-esim/) solves it in the two minutes between landing and clearing passport control, and costs a few euro. Doing it that way, rather than queuing at a SIM desk in arrivals, is the difference between opening a taxi app on the kerb and standing there hoping for airport wifi.

### Pre-booked transfers

A private transfer is the middle ground: a fixed price agreed in advance, a driver holding a sign, and no decisions to make when you are tired.

For Tirana itself, <a class="affil" href="https://tp.media/r?campaign_id=627&marker=770798&p=8919&trs=567396&u=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.welcomepickups.com%2Ftirana%2F&sub_id=tirana-airport-transport" target="_blank" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener">Welcome Pickups</a> works on roughly the same price level as the official rank but with an English-speaking local driver and a flight-tracked pickup, so a delayed landing does not cost you the booking. That last part is the real reason to pre-book rather than improvise.

Where a transfer earns its money properly is the long haul. If you are landing at Rinas and sleeping in Sarandë, that is a four-hour drive and there is no direct public option after the early buses have gone. A <a class="affil" href="https://tp.media/r?campaign_id=1&marker=770798&p=647&trs=567396&u=https%3A%2F%2Fkiwitaxi.com%2Fen%2Falbania&sub_id=tirana-airport-transport" target="_blank" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener">booked long-distance transfer</a> is expensive next to a bus ticket and cheap next to a wasted first day, and for three or four people splitting it, the arithmetic gets reasonable quickly.

### Renting at arrivals

The rental desks are in the arrivals hall, and the international chains all have a presence. They also charge airport rates.

Albanian agencies are generally cheaper, more flexible about the deposit, and many will meet you at arrivals or deliver to your hotel; most are listed on <a class="affil" href="https://tp.media/r?campaign_id=87&marker=770798&p=2043&trs=567396&u=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.localrent.com%2Fen%2Falbania%2F&sub_id=tirana-airport-transport" target="_blank" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener">Localrent</a>, where you can see the deposit terms next to the daily rate before committing.

One honest warning: **do not collect a car at the airport if your first stop is central Tirana.** Tirana parking is genuinely painful, the ring road is chaotic, and you will pay for a car to sit still. Collect it on the day you leave the city. I have written separately about [what renting a car in Albania actually costs](/blog/renting-a-car-in-albania/), including the deposit traps, and about [why the roads are the real risk here](/blog/is-albania-safe/).

## Going Somewhere That Is Not Tirana

This is the part most airport guides skip.

**Durrës** is 40 minutes away and there are direct buses from the airport, from around 6 euro. If your holiday is the coast and not the capital, you never need to enter Tirana at all.

**Shkodër** has had a direct airport service since 2024, four departures a day covering the 100 kilometres in about ninety minutes. If you are heading for the Alps, the Koman ferry or [the Valbona to Theth hike](/blog/valbona-to-theth-hike/), this saves you a pointless detour through the capital.

**Everywhere else in the south**, meaning Berat, Vlorë, Himarë, Sarandë, Gjirokastër, Përmet, runs from Tirana's terminals rather than the airport. The airport bus conveniently passes near the combined North and South terminal on its way in, so you can get off there, but check with the driver rather than assuming. From those terminals the [furgon network](/blog/how-to-use-furgons-in-albania/) will take you almost anywhere in the country.

## The Other Two Airports

Rinas is no longer Albania's only way in, though it is still the only one most travellers will use.

**Kukës International Airport** opened in 2021 in the northeast, with a thin and frequently changing schedule of diaspora routes. It is a genuinely useful arrival point for the Alps and for Kosovo, and almost nobody outside the diaspora knows it exists.

**Vlora International Airport** flew its certification flights in 2025 and is the one to watch, since it puts the Riviera within half an hour of an arrivals hall. Be careful with the promises, though. As things stand for 2026 the confirmed scheduled service is Chair Airlines from Zurich, running from late June to late October only. Outside that window there is no scheduled commercial traffic. Plan on flying into Tirana and treat Vlora as a bonus if your dates and origin happen to line up.

## Going Home

Two hours before departure for a European flight, and I mean it in July and August. The terminal was built for a much smaller Albania than the one currently arriving, and summer mornings back up badly.

Coming from central Tirana, the bus leaves from the same stop behind the opera house, hourly, around the clock. Allow 45 minutes for the ride rather than 25, because the airport road at eight in the morning is not the airport road at midnight.

And if you are returning a rental car, fill the tank before the airport turn-off. The stations closest to Rinas know exactly where you are going.

## The Short Version

Take the bus. It runs all night, it costs four euro, and it puts you two minutes from Skanderbeg Square.

Download an Albanian taxi app before you land, because Uber and Bolt are not coming and the rank price is fixed high.

Pre-book a transfer if you are arriving at an awkward hour, travelling with people who cannot easily wait, or going straight to the far south.

And leave the rental car until the day you actually leave Tirana. The city will not reward you for having one.
