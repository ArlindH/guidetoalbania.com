Most of what you will read about the cost of Albania was written between 2018 and 2022, and it is wrong now. Not because Albanian prices exploded, though tourist-facing ones have certainly climbed, but because of something quieter that almost nobody writing about the country has accounted for.

**The lek got strong.** A decade ago a euro bought you around 140 lekë, and as recently as 2021 it still bought more than 120. As of August 2026 it buys about 92. If your money is euros, that means every unchanged Albanian price tag became roughly a third more expensive to you in a few years without a single number on it moving.

This is the honest starting point for any budget conversation about Albania in 2026. It is still cheaper than Italy, Greece or Croatia, comfortably. It is no longer the country where a hundred euro lasts most of a week.

## The Headline Numbers

Per person, per day, excluding flights.

| | Daily budget | What that looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Backpacker** | 30 to 45 EUR | Hostel dorm, byrek and street food, furgons, one paid sight |
| **Mid-range** | 70 to 110 EUR | Guesthouse or 3-star double, restaurant meals, shared car or occasional taxi |
| **Comfortable** | 150 EUR and up | Boutique hotel, wine with dinner, private transfers, guided days |

Two people travelling mid-range together will spend roughly 110 to 170 euro a day combined, because the double room is the single biggest line and it does not double.

That mid-range band is where most visitors actually land, and it is worth saying plainly: **that is a real European travel budget now.** It is perhaps forty percent below the equivalent week in Greece, not ninety.

## Where the Money Actually Goes

After a decade of watching visitors do this, the pattern is consistent. Three line items eat most of a budget, and only one of them is obvious.

### 1. Accommodation in July and August

Riviera prices in the first three weeks of August are not Albanian prices, they are seasonal scarcity prices, and they can triple. The same apartment in Himarë that costs 45 euro in June costs 130 in August.

**This is the single biggest lever you have.** Move your trip to early June or the second half of September and the entire budget drops by a quarter, the water is still warm, and you can park. I have written elsewhere about [when to actually visit](/blog/best-time-to-visit-albania-2/), and the money argument is the strongest one in it.

### 2. Transport, and specifically the car question

A rental car is the largest discretionary decision in an Albanian budget. Off season an economy manual runs 10 to 20 euro a day. In August, booked late, the same car is 25 to 40, and automatics are both scarcer and dearer.

Add fuel at roughly two euro a litre, and a week of driving lands somewhere between 200 and 400 euro all in.

Is it worth it? Often yes, because the places that repay you most in Albania are the ones public transport reaches worst. But be honest about your route. If you are doing Tirana, Berat and Sarandë, [the furgon network](/blog/how-to-use-furgons-in-albania/) will do it for under 40 euro total and you will not be paying for a car to sit outside a hotel for three days.

If you do want one, book it early for summer and compare deposit terms rather than headline rates. ### 3. The invisible costs

These are the ones that ruin a carefully planned budget.

**Roaming.** Albania is not in the EU roaming zone, and a fortnight of your phone behaving normally can cost more than your accommodation. Sort it before you fly. A few euro on an <a class="affil" href="https://tp.media/r?campaign_id=541&marker=770798&p=8310&trs=567396&u=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.airalo.com%2Falbania-esim&sub_id=albania-travel-cost-budget" target="_blank" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener">Albanian eSIM</a> against a possible three-figure bill is the easiest arithmetic in this article, and I have set out [all the connectivity options here](/blog/albania-sim-card-esim/).

**ATM fees.** Albanian ATMs charge for withdrawals, commonly 500 to 650 lekë a time, so five or six euro. Taking out 5,000 lekë at a time and paying that fee repeatedly is a genuinely stupid way to lose 30 euro over a fortnight. **Withdraw large amounts, rarely.** And always decline the machine's offer to convert to your home currency; that "convenience" costs several percent.

**Insurance.** Albanian public healthcare is thin outside Tirana, private care is billed on the spot, and the road accident rate is high enough that [I have written a whole article about it](/blog/is-albania-safe/). If you are driving, hiking, or on the water, a policy that actually covers those things is not optional. A week of <a class="affil" href="https://tp.media/r?campaign_id=225&marker=770798&p=5869&trs=567396&u=https%3A%2F%2Fektatraveling.com%2F&sub_id=albania-travel-cost-budget" target="_blank" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener">cover from EKTA</a> costs less than one dinner on the Riviera.

## How to Actually Spend Less

Not tips, habits. These are what the difference between a 45 euro day and a 90 euro day is actually made of.

**Pay in lekë. Always.** Menus and rental desks that quote euro are using a rate that is quietly in their favour. Restaurants, kiosks, furgons and markets all price in lekë and the exchange is worse every time you let someone else do it for you.

**Eat where the plastic chairs are.** A 500 lekë lunch and a 2,000 lekë dinner are frequently the same food. The difference is the tablecloth and the position on the promenade.

**Drink the local wine.** Albanian wine at 350 lekë a glass is a genuine discovery. Imported Italian at 900 is not.

**Take the furgon at least once.** Beyond the money, it is one of the few remaining ways to see how the country actually moves.

**Skip Ksamil in August.** I say this with some feeling. It is beautiful and it is now priced and crowded like a resort. [The southern coast has better options](/blog/must-visit-southern-albanian-beaches-top-riviera-locations/) at half the price.

**Do not tip like an American.** Rounding up is normal, ten percent is generous, and nobody is offended by either. Leaving twenty percent distorts local expectations and does not make you a better guest.

## A Real Week, Costed

Two people, mid-range, nine nights in late September. Tirana, Berat, the Riviera, Gjirokastër. No car for the first three days, a hired one after that.

| | EUR |
|---|---|
| Beds, 9 nights | 480 |
| Food and drink, 2 people | 560 |
| Car, 6 days including fuel | 290 |
| Furgons and airport bus | 35 |
| Entry tickets and one guide | 95 |
| eSIM and insurance | 45 |
| **Total for two** | **1,505** |

That is roughly 84 euro per person per day, which is a fair mid-range figure for 2026 and about what I would tell a friend to plan for.

Run the same trip in the second week of August and the accommodation line alone rises by 300 euro. Run it as a backpacker on furgons in October and two people will do it for 700.

Albania rewards flexibility more than almost anywhere I know. The month you choose matters more than every saving tip in this article put together.
